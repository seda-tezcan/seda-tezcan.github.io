The central idea

An opponent with a visible rule.

Every five moves, surviving dice flip to their opposite face. The player can turn that reversal into an opportunity by choosing which target to finish or preserve.

Read the system before adding to it

Three insights that shaped the proposal

The analysis became useful when it explained what the player had to decide.

Space matters as much as moves

Board area stayed broadly similar across the reviewed periods, while unblocked working space declined. A tighter workspace changes where useful groups and specials can form.

Timing can give a target two values

Periodic threats turn a target into a choice about when to act. The new mechanic should make that timing visible and predictable.

A climax can arrive before completion

In a selected playthrough, the major enemies were gone while collection goals remained. Feedback should continue to communicate the real remaining objective.

The middle period had a higher average move budget than the opening period. I did not interpret the progression as a steady decrease in moves, or use obstacle density alone as a difficulty score.

Snake Eyes & Loaded Dice

A casino proprietor changes every surviving die to its opposite face every five completed moves. The visible face is the number of hits left.

Teach the reversal with a readable board

The tutorial isolates six dice on an open 7 × 7 board. Its guided opening demonstrates a permanent clear, a recovering target and a weakening target.

Tutorial design

Predict first. Explain afterwards.

A and B demonstrate opposite outcomes at the first flip. E is cleared before the reversal and does not return. The remaining targets give the player a reason to continue after guidance.

The key learning test is whether a player can predict the next face and explain why one target should be finished while another is left alone.

Output and next validation

The design connects structural analysis to a new player decision and a specified tutorial. It has not been validated through a reported human study.

100levels reviewed
18obstacle families classified
5guided moves scripted

The output is a character concept, a deterministic obstacle specification, an authored tutorial board and a playtest plan. The proposal is strongest where it makes the reversal understandable and gives both acting and waiting a reason.

The main risks are recovering durability feeling like erased progress, and cascades changing a face before the player’s predicted flip. Countdown feedback, a face preview and the guided opening are intended mitigations; their effect remains to be tested.

PropertySpecification

DurabilityVisible face 1–6 equals remaining hits

SpecialOne hit per distinct activation; overlapping cells do not multiply it
CascadesSeparate match events can cause separate damage
RemovalImmediate at zero; permanent; no replacement die spawns
Resolution order on the fifth move
  1. Accept a valid move and spend it.
  2. Resolve matches, specials, cascades, damage, removal and refill.
  3. Complete the countdown. If the last die was cleared, cancel the flip.
  4. Read each surviving die’s resulting face and replace it with 7 minus that value.
  5. Show the reversal, reset the countdown and reopen input.

Invalid swaps do not advance the meter. Cascades do not count as extra moves.

Tutorial guardrails
  • Introduce the damage rule before the reversal rule.
  • Script the opening refills to avoid an accidental cascade teaching a different result.
  • Demonstrate both recovery and weakening, plus a clear that never returns.
  • Keep later goals visible after the character is defeated.
First playtest questions
  • Can players predict two opposite-face changes before the first flip?
  • Can they explain the timer and reversal without help after guidance?
  • When a suitable choice exists, do they deliberately finish a low die or preserve a high die, and explain why?
  • Where do unexpected cascades, retries or unclear feedback interrupt that understanding?

Completion alone would not establish understanding. For a small test, report participant counts with any percentages.
