A calm craft.
A planning puzzle.

The intended experience combines the soothing feeling of making something with the satisfaction of planning well. Selected yarn becomes a scarf, beanie, rug or blanket, stitch by stitch. The initial design has no time pressure.

Audience hypothesis: people playing in short breaks who enjoy organising, planning resources and seeing the visible results of their choices. This audience assumption has not yet been validated.

How one choice changes the next

The player manages three connected resources: the amount of yarn, access to the shelf and free working space.

Choose from the front

Only the front spool in each shelf column is selectable. Taking it into one of two working slots reveals the spool behind it.

Knit the current row

Yarn automatically fills cells of its own colour in the active row. A row may require several colours, used in any order. The next row starts only when the current one is complete.

Carry the remainder

An empty spool frees its slot. Leftover yarn stays in the slot for a later row, preserving a resource while occupying limited space.

A meaningful choice · Same colour, different consequences

The shorter spool

It may empty on the current row, freeing a working slot and exposing a needed spool behind it.

The longer spool

It supplies the same colour now, but its remaining yarn may occupy a slot until a future row needs it.

Which spool helps now—and leaves room for what comes next?

What changed through prototyping

I developed the concept, rules and levels, made visual feedback decisions and iterated on the prototype. Development used ChatGPT assistance, beginning on the web and then moving to Unity / C#.

  1. From single colours to mixed rows

    Rows evolved from one colour to several, adding a choice about which available yarn to use first.

  2. From written counts to countable cells

    Instead of asking players to interpret “two red needed,” each yarn unit maps to one visible target cell. The aim is to make quantities easier to read.

  3. From a pattern to a crafted object

    Textured scarves, beanies, rugs and blankets give completion a tangible payoff.

  4. From an instant reveal to visible making

    Stitch-by-stitch animation follows the transformation from yarn to product, connecting the chosen resource to its result.

  5. From web exploration to Unity

    The Unity prototype now contains nine levels, including a new five-colour level with green and pink. These are authored prototype levels; their player experience still needs testing.

The game screen’s visual design is still in progress. This page presents the design reasoning and prototype scope.

The question to test next

Does managing yarn quantity and remaining space feel like enjoyable planning, or tiring bookkeeping?

A solvable level establishes that a completion path exists. It does not establish that players understand the rules, enjoy the decisions or want another level. User testing is still planned.

  • Can players explain why leftover yarn remains in a slot?
  • Do they look ahead to later colour needs?
  • Can they explain why they became stuck?
  • After undoing, do they form a different plan?
